## Consequences for publication claims

1. **Do not publish** "first calculated charged scalar squirkonium rates",
   "first helicity analysis of WZ", "new cos(phi) angular interference", or
   "first bound-state spin-discriminator". Primary sources refute those
   general claims.
2. The only remaining candidate in this branch is a **model-specific,
   sign-sensitive correlation** between erratum-restricted mass splitting
   and the observable signed longitudinal–transverse interference moment a1,
   possibly accompanied by a controlled comparison against a physically
   well-specified competing bound state. It must produce discriminating
   information not already in rate, f00, or generic H-to-VV angular formalism.
3. A serious feasibility issue is that a1 has a factor
   (gR^2-gL^2)/(gR^2+gL^2) from leptonic Z decay; experiment-level reach is
   not established. Any comparative prediction must preserve W/Z decay-frame
   sign conventions, physical spin/parity, and charged-state populations.
4. A second physical fermionic quirk model may carry different J^P for the
   lightest bound state. A difference in angular coefficients may measure
   spin/parity of the *bound state*, not uniquely the constituent spins.
   Treat this as a model-comparison caveat, not a validated discriminator.
5. Do not claim novelty from a negative keyword search. The complete
   INSPIRE forward/backward citation graph, extra theses, and all generic
   charged-resonance analyses were **not** exhaustively covered in this pass.

## 2026-11-07 continuation: explicit two-branch interference mechanism

The physics pilot has identified a narrowly scoped potential result:
the published tree-level scalar quirkonium WZ and Wgamma rates imply
Gamma(WZ)/Gamma(Wgamma) =
[tan^2(thetaW) beta_WZ / beta_Wgamma]/(1-f00).
At fixed M, the WZ/Wgamma normalized rates and f00 thus carry the
same information; the longitudinal/transverse relative phase does not
appear in either. The ideal lepton decay-plane signed interference
a1 proportional to Re(H++ H00*) can distinguish amplitude branches
sharing the same f00.

For M=500 GeV, tanbeta=3 and tanbeta_low approximately 0.394724021524:
f00=0.9092814481, BR(WZ) under the two-channel assumption=0.7530816453,
while a1 approximately -0.1203051105 and +0.1203051105 respectively.
The discrete sign ambiguity is a **mathematical LO model feature**,
not yet demonstrated physically realizable in the original SUSY
construction. The 2025 paper discusses tanbeta>1 in its SUSY context;
the 2026 erratum plots a wider interval 0.1--10. Both facts must be
reconciled before claiming a viable low-tanbeta branch.

This is more specific than generic cosPhi/cos2Phi angles, which are
known from diboson resonance angular analyses; novelty still UNKNOWN.
The external MG5 helicity-summed matrix-element benchmark is scoped
PASS at M=500, tanbeta=3, but independent complex amplitude phases and
multi-benchmark pass are PENDING. Nor has the user yet run new ideal

### Source clarification and additional hard limitation — 2026 erratum

The 2026 erratum explicitly describes the range 0.1 <= tanbeta <= 10
as effectively covering its *physically meaningful* D-term-splitting
parameter space and presents bounds separately for tanbeta<1 and >1;
see Forsyth et al., PRD 113, 079901 (2026), DOI:10.1103/lwnc-cmpn,
corrected Eq. (3.4), Figs. 4–6. This strengthens the justification
for **considering** a tanbeta_low=0.395 branch within the simplified
phenomenological treatment. But the original 2025 source introduces
tanbeta as the SUSY Higgs VEV ratio with tanbeta>1 (Sec. 3, original
text around Eq. 3.2). A UV-complete or phenomenologically qualified
two-branch model remains UNKNOWN and must not be silently assumed.

The two solutions match f00 and **normalized BR(WZ) under the assumed
two-channel ground state**, NOT generally the absolute Gamma(WZ) or
Gamma(Wgamma): those rates can differ via D(Delta) and through the
unknown bound-state wavefunction. No rate-/cross-section-based
statistical likelihood or physically matched production claim has
been made. A competing-model contrast must take this into account.
